> **Rev 21 · 2026-09-09** — one decision-log entry **added**, and it is a *rejection*: **organ-level damage is not the model**. A RimWorld-style proposal (≈28 organs, each with its own HP, mapped under the hit/clothing locations, with organs wired to functions like breathing and consciousness, plus triage, surgery and transplant) was evaluated against Rev 20's just-locked wound model and declined. It is recorded rather than dropped because the *idea* is good and will be proposed again — the argument for declining is what has to survive, not the outcome. The short form: Legend's Serious and Major Wound rules already deliver every behaviour the organ layer was wanted for — function loss, drop-what-you-hold, prone, unconsciousness, a `CON+POW` bleed-out clock and *"most Major Wounds require some form of surgery or major magic"* — so the layer adds **nouns, not decisions**, at the cost of a third state model beneath a taxonomy whose *second* one is still an open sub-question under Rev 20 (armour AP ↔ `ContribClothing` slots is unsolved at seven locations; twenty-eight multiplies it). **What is kept** is the part that was actually new: the split between **stabilising in the field and repairing afterwards**, which is filed in `docs/BACKLOG.md` (*Health & Injury*, Rev 29) rather than here, because it is a feature smaller than a stage. No sequencing changes; Rev 20's model stands unamended.
> **Rev 20 · 2026-09-09** — Stage 5 gains its first **locked** design decision: **hit locations & wound permanence**. This document has cited hit locations as Stage 5 Legend-alignment since Rev 1 without ever choosing the *data model* behind them, and the model is not a combat detail — it is read by the shipped `apply_health_damage()` chokepoint, by per-location armour AP, and by the `ContribClothing` slot taxonomy, so it is expensive to retrofit once the epic is underway. It lands **now, between 4.5's close and Stage 5 planning**, which is the only window where it costs a document edit rather than a migration. Decided: **asymmetric HP** (locational for players, Legend's own optional **General Hit Points + Critical Wound** rule for creatures and NPCs), **three wound tiers** (disabled → maimed → severed), and **permanence feature-gated at ship** — the severed tier is implemented but switched off until a cure exists, because a world with no healers and no magic would permanently maim its first cohort with no route out. Gating rather than deferring is the point: the retrofit is what costs, not the flag. Two constraints are recorded because they are easy to violate silently — locational HP must permit **negative** values (Major Wound is *defined* as negative HP equal to the location's starting HP, and `CounterTrait` clamps at `min` without saying so), and lasting wound state is **not** a buff (`buffs.add()` stacks rather than sets, and a buff is semantically temporary). Four sub-questions stay open under the entry, and one of them is **not this entry's to answer**: whether wounds replace or coexist with `DeathWeakness` belongs to the **[OPEN] death-consequence policy**, which Rev 19 already declared due *before* Stage 5 planning. Deciding it here would have been half a player contract settled in passing. No sequencing changes; combat-first stands.

### Stage 1 — Skill Improvement System ✅ *(complete — merged to `main`)*
**Goal (met):** an automated, Legend-faithful progression layer so skills grow through use — no GM, no levels, no XP-as-a-character-stat — **and is *felt*.** Because Legend has no level-up "ding," a mechanically correct system can still ship as an invisible backend that feels dead. The epic is not done when the number quietly grows; it's done when the player *notices* growth. This epic owns the skill-number axis **and its presentation**.
**Why here (before combat):** relatively contained, but it retroactively makes *all* existing skill use — hunting, crafting, foraging — progression-meaningful at once, and combat + magic will both lean on it. Highest leverage per unit effort on the board.
**Legend alignment:** Legend has **no character levels and no XP**. Its two advancement paths port very differently:
- *Improvement Rolls* — GM-awarded at narrative beats (roll 1D100 + INT vs current skill → >current gives +1D4+1, else +1). The real trigger is the **GM's judgment of a story beat**, which has no multiplayer equivalent, so this path **does not port**. Replaced with **improvement-on-use that resolves at the check itself** (RuneScape-style): immediate feedback, rewards activity. ✅ shipped.
- *Training* — downtime + teacher + funds; a week of study, then 1D100 vs current skill; can't train the same skill twice in a row. This **ports cleanly** (classic MUD trainer tradition). The teacher is a *player* with high skill + Teaching charging coin — a progression sink *and* an economic activity (pillar 1). *(Deferred; pairs with Stage 3 recipe-teaching and Stage 4 currency.)*

**Anti-grind throttle (shipped):** the action's own cooldown + Legend's self-throttling curve (high skills rise slowly) — **not** forced rest. Improvement is gated to success-against-real-difficulty under a per-skill real-time cooldown.
**Felt-progress / legibility layer (✅ delivered):** immediate feedback on each meaningful tick, celebration on crossing a skill's **named desc-tier boundaries**, and the `progress` command showing deltas since login. This is what converts "the number grew" into "I made progress." *(Tactical detail — command, message copy, tier logic — lives in the Stage 1 decomposition, Rev 3.)*
**Outcome (see decision log):** raw % is the single mechanical truth; surfaced RuneScape-near via frequent on-use ticks + desc-tier-crossing celebration; no cosmetic 1–99 badge for now.
**Pillars:** progression backbone for every skill-using system; the Training path (deferred) doubles as player-to-player economic activity (pillar 1).

### Stage 2 — Crafting progression & tools ✅ *(complete — merged to `main`)*
**Goal:** turn Stage 1's skill numbers into **felt capability** — gate higher recipes behind skill thresholds, scale output quality with skill (crit-craft → superior item) — and make tools a player-crafted quality/efficiency layer with a durability sink.
**Why here (promoted ahead of currency):** it depends only on Stage 1 (now done) and is the cheapest, highest-"makes-progression-matter" payoff on the board. Without it the Stage 1 numbers stay a stat readout; with it they become a growing craft menu and better goods.
**Scope — two threads:**
- *Skill → capability:* recipe skill-gates + quality tiers driven by the craft-check outcome (the fumble/success/critical tiers `skillcheck.py` already resolves). Rides straight on the improvement layer.
- *Tools:* tools must be player-crafted (no implicit NPC source, per pillar 1). Design them as quality/efficiency **modifiers, not hard gates** (fits the existing `consume_policy="raw"` philosophy), to avoid the bootstrap chicken-and-egg where the first tool can't be made. **Tool durability/wear = the sink**, and creates recurring economic demand.
**Bonus:** individuating crafted items by material/quality ("a superior steel dagger") also feeds the search/disambiguation root-cause fix (backlog) for free.
**Dependencies:** Stage 1 (✅), crafting foundation (✅).
**Pillars:** player-driven economy (recurring tool demand = sink), progression (felt capability).
**Rough scope:** small–moderate — a refinement of the existing crafting system, not a from-scratch epic.

### Stage 3 — Recipe knowledge & discovery ✅ *(complete — merged to `main`)*
**Goal:** make recipe knowledge a **gated, tradeable resource** rather than a universal capability (decision log: RESOLVED). Today every character can craft every recipe but can't even *see* which exist; knowledge is neither gated nor a resource. Players learn / buy / sell / teach recipes → knowledge becomes an economic good driving specialisation and interdependence (pillar 1).
**Why here (before currency):** pillar-1-core, and it pairs tightly with Stage 1's training loop (teaching) and Stage 2's skill gate. Buy/sell ideally wants coin, but **barter works in the interim** — so it can precede currency without blocking.
**Keep three orthogonal gates distinct:** **knowledge** (binary — do you know it? *this epic*), **skill** (how good — Stage 1 + Stage 2's quality scaling), **capability** (tools/stations — Stage 2 tools / deferred metallurgy).
**Scope:** a per-character known-recipe set; a craft-time knowledge check; a discovery/legibility surface (what you know, with a hint that more exists); and knowledge *sources* — tradeable books/scrolls, player teaching (matches Stage 1's training loop **and** Legend's teacher-gate: Craft can't be self-taught, rulebook p.70–71), and profession grants at chargen.
**Cold-start hybrid (near-certain):** basic survival recipes = common knowledge; only advanced recipes must be learned — else new players can't eat or make twine (the same cold-start shape the GameGold temple-faucet solves).
**Bootstrap concern:** books/teachers need their *own* first source (world-loot seed or a starting master), or no one can write the first book.
**Legend fidelity:** "recipes" are a MUD convention (Legend has only Craft rolls), but gated knowledge *transfer via teacher* is rulebook-faithful.
**Dependencies:** crafting foundation (✅); pairs with **Stage 1** (teaching = the training loop), **Stage 2** (the skill gate), and **Stage 4** (buy/sell wants real coin — barter interim).
**Open sub-decisions (its design session):** book vs scroll (sink strength); exact cold-start baseline; bootstrap source (world-loot seed vs starting master vs profession grants); whether player teaching requires Legend's Teaching skill. See decision log.
**Pillars:** player-driven economy (knowledge as a tradeable good; specialisation + interdependence).
**Rough scope:** moderate–large. **Needs its own design session** before decomposition.

### Stage 4 — In-game currency ✅ *(complete — merged to `main`)*
**Goal:** Gold/Silver/Copper as actual money (100:1:1), with a character wallet and basic give/pay/price plumbing — the medium of exchange the economy currently lacks.
**Why here:** the economy is **barter-only** today (no coin system in the repo). Several items assume money: Stage 1's Training-via-teacher, Stage 3's recipe buy/sell (charging coin), and **Stage 8 GameGold, which is defined as 1:1 with in-game gold** — gold must exist as a currency before the crypto layer can bridge to it. Kept small and early so it unblocks all coin-based trade while the economy is still small.
**Pillars:** player-driven economy (the exchange primitive everything else trades through).
**Dependencies:** none hard; pairs with barter (`PWTradeHandler`), Stage 1's training loop, and Stage 3's recipe trade.
**Hard requirement:** must land **before Stage 8 (GameGold)**.
**Rough scope:** small, ~3–5 commits.
**Design notes:** *(both questions now answered — kept for the reasoning.)* Denominations are a single base-unit integer under the hood (stored in Copper, rendered as G/S/C), which is what makes rounding bugs structurally impossible rather than merely guarded against — **S4-2**. Wallet-on-character won over coin-as-objects for Stage 4: an integer Attribute, with no `wallet` Attribute declared on the typeclass so there is no bypass write (**D6**), and no coin objects at all. Coin therefore reaches the barter table as a number on the trade handler rather than as goods. Coin-as-objects returns as `CoinPile` in **Stage 5**, where it is a death-drop question rather than a wallet question.
**Shipped:** `world/currency.py`, `world/economy_log.py`, `typeclasses/treasury.py`, `wallet`/`pay`/`@economy`/`work`, and the barter currency bridge. 316 tests. Components A–F; see `docs/PolishedWorld_Currency_Decomposition.md`.

### ✅ Stage 4.5 — Skill progression (XP) — **COMPLETE 2026-08-24** *(small; blocks nothing, shapes everything)*
**Goal:** give every skill a growth curve that survives a persistent world, by reinterpreting Legend's Improvement Roll rather than replacing it. The roll stays verbatim; its *output* becomes XP banked toward the next whole percentage point instead of points added directly.
**Why here:** Stage 1 shipped a mechanically faithful system that is **43 successful uses wide**. Measured at INT 12: 0 → 100 in about 22 minutes of pure cooldown, under two hours including gathering. Every epic after this one — combat, magic, wilderness — hangs progression off the same primitive, so the curve is far cheaper to fix now than once three more systems ride on it. It also blocks nothing: no other stage depends on it.
**Scope — the shared primitive, so all six improvement call-sites move together:** craft, repair, hunt-attack, hunt-harvest, disassemble, scribe. Crafting-only would leave hunting maxing in 43 kills, and two progression engines in one game. *(Rev 15: five → six. Scribe rolls Craft with a material cost and a real failure mode; it was left out by omission, and the resulting rule — some Craft rolls teach, some do not, and the player is not told which — is not one anybody could infer.)*
**Storage:** lifetime-total XP per skill is the sole persisted truth. Level and progress bar are **derived**, never stored — so recalibrating is a settings change rather than a migration, and the bar cannot drift out of step because there is nothing to drift. `skill.current` becomes a materialised cache with exactly one writer, the same discipline `world/currency.py` holds over the wallet Attribute (**S4-R2**).
**Curve shape (measured, not assumed):** normalised to equal total playtime, Tibia's cubic and a doubling-every-20-points exponential put **68%** and **70%** of playtime in the last quartile of the scale; RuneScape's (×2 per 7 levels) puts **94%** there, which on a 100-point scale erases the lower half entirely. RuneScape's shape is ruled out; the other two are effectively the same design.
**Calibration is deliberately NOT fixed by this epic.** It cannot be known before there is content and players. One documented constant in `settings.py` carries a provisional value plus the procedure to recompute it. **Generous now, tightened never** — lowering the curve later is free, raising it de-levels people.
**Cap:** Legend's >100% band, which `world/improvement.py` deliberately left unimplemented (the formula is already sitting in its docstring), becomes reachable. No hard cap at 100.
**⛔ Component E (difficulty gate) did not ship, and has been moved out of the epic (2026-08-24).** Without it the optimal route to Craft 100 is to repeat the cheapest recipe you know ~2 900 times; the curve taxes that grind but does not object to it. The gate that fixes it — a craft far below your skill teaches nothing, which is Legend-faithful and uses a seam `attempt_skill_improvement` already carries — needs recipes at graduated `min_skill` values. There are eight recipes and the highest is 30, so the gate would stop teaching Craft above 61 and strand every crafter who reached it. **Trigger: a recipe catalogue that ladders across the scale** (`docs/BACKLOG.md`, *Crafting & Tools*, BLOCKED — now E's only armed home). A–D shipped without it. It was moved out rather than left inside a closing epic because the trigger belongs to no scheduled stage: a stage held open by an unscheduled trigger blocks the next one silently, and Stage 5 must not start behind a component nobody has scheduled.
**Measured effect (2026-08-05, 400 simulated careers, INT 10):** craft 20 → 100 takes **38** eligible ticks on the shipped system and **2 931** after Component C — a 77× slowdown, or ~19 minutes against ~24 hours at the 30 s cooldown. The cooldown is deliberately **frozen** at 30 and reclassified from anti-spam guard to wall-clock floor: the curve is the sole calibration knob, because two levers with the same observable effect cannot be told apart after the fact.
**Delivered (2026-08-24):** A (curve as pure functions), B (XP store + read-time level fallback), C (the primitive banks XP instead of adding points), D (derived progress bar; Legend's >100% band, cap removed). **429 tests green**, in-game verified. Two findings worth carrying forward: `skill.current` is a materialised cache and every out-of-band write to it suspends P-1's direction — the epic's own test protocols manufactured that state twice and produced two false claims doing it; and **five production comments were falsified** by mechanisms changing underneath them, all the same shape (prose asserting what a mechanism guarantees, left standing after the mechanism changed). Prose has no tests, so a grep loop over the retired wording is now part of the close-out (Testing Reference Rev 7 §11c).
**Dependencies:** Stage 1 (✅). **Pillars:** progression backbone for every skill-using system.
**Rough scope:** small–moderate; **delivered in 8 commits** across A–D (A and B took one each; E moved out and is not counted). Decomp: `PolishedWorld_Skill_Progression_Decomposition.md` (Rev 8).

### Stage 5 — Combat *(recommended big epic)*
**Goal:** opposed-d100 combat that turns the survival *simulation* into a survival *game* with stakes.
**Why here:**
- Hunting **Stage 0 already provides the seams** (`at_character_death()`, `apply_health_damage()` chokepoint) — combat plugs straight in.
- It gives **existing crafting a purpose**: weapons and armor currently have no use.
- It's the **highest-leverage gameplay addition** for the few early players.

**Mongoose Legend alignment:** opposed d100, combat actions, hit locations, fumble/critical tiers (already in `skillcheck.py`), weapons/armor from *Arms of Legend*. **Key adaptation decision:** real-time-with-cooldowns vs turn-based rounds — project stance is real-time/cooldowns, which needs careful multiplayer-fairness design.
**Locked (Rev 20) — hit locations & wound model:** asymmetric HP (locational for players, General HP + Critical Wound for creatures/NPCs), three wound tiers (disabled / maimed / severed), permanence feature-gated until a cure exists. Full entry and its four open sub-questions in the decision log.
**Dependencies:** Stage 0 (death seams), crafting (gear), thermal/clothing (armor-layering interaction to resolve — now sharpened by the hit-location decision: the `ContribClothing` slot taxonomy and Legend's seven Hit Locations are two different taxonomies that must be mapped, and AP stacking across layers needs source verification in *Arms of Legend*).
**Pillars:** survival (stakes), player-driven economy (activates weapon/armor sinks).
**Rough scope:** large, ~10–15+ commits. **Needs its own design/source-verification session** (Legend combat + *Arms of Legend*) before decomposition.
**Open questions:** PvE-only first or PvP? Cooldown model for fairness under 10+ concurrent players. Where does non-combat damage (cold, starvation) land now that damage is located — Legend routes drowning to Chest *and* Head, so a policy is required rather than a default?

### Stage 6 — Wilderness / XYZ grid + dynamic world scaling
**Goal:** give the world Daggerfall-scale room; tie world growth to population milestones (story-justified frontier setting).
**Why here (after combat):** new space populated with *stakes* (encounters, danger) rather than empty rooms; more room for hunting/foraging/combat.
**Implementation note:** source-verified 2026-08-14 — see `PolishedWorld_Evennia_Reference.md` §14 (read against Evennia `main` and diffed against the pinned `v6.1.0` tag; identical). Working shape is **two contribs side by side**: `evennia.contrib.grid.xyzgrid` for authored persistent structure (roads, villages) and `evennia.contrib.grid.wilderness` for large homogeneous resource areas (forest, mountain). They have separate coordinate spaces and **no built-in bridge** — grid↔wilderness transitions are project code at trailhead rooms. The shape is not yet a locked decision; see the open questions below. **Procedural generation is the long-term vision — initial scope is a hand-built frontier with scaling hooks**, not a generator.
**Dependencies:** creature spawning (done), combat (so wilderness has stakes), gametime bridge.
**Pillars:** dynamic environment, dynamic world scaling.
**Rough scope:** large.
**⚠️ Main sequencing fork:** Stage 5 ↔ Stage 6 order. Recommendation is combat-first (Stage 0 seams + crafting payoff). Flip to world-first only if "more space" feels more compelling to early players than "more danger." Record the decision below when made.
**Open questions (all unlocked — source reading produced candidates, not decisions):**
1. **Shared room base.** Neither `XYZRoom` nor `WildernessRoom` inherits `ExtendedRoom`, so weather, seasonal descs and room states don't come along. Candidate is a mixin consumed by both sides; the grid side composes for free, the wilderness side needs the *behaviour* re-implemented in memory because its *storage* (Tags/Attributes on a recycled shell) leaks between coordinates (Reference §14.5, §14.7).
2. **Resources: coordinate-data vs objects-in-rooms.** The wilderness contrib's per-step lookup is O(total objects in the wilderness) (Reference §14.6), which argues for data materialised into an object only on harvest. That conflicts with how `ResourceNode` works today, and with tag-based creature spawning — both assume a room that survives being left. *(Interim rule until decided: decision log, Stage 6 entry — Rev 27.)*
3. **Building workflow for grid areas.** `xyzgrid spawn` deletes rooms off-map and overwrites prototype-defined attributes (Reference §14.2), so grid areas are authored in versioned map modules rather than in-game. Habit change, and the command is destructive.
4. **Sub-sequencing within the stage.** Grid-first is the candidate — smaller, lower risk, and it yields in-game minimap and pathfinding immediately (Reference §14.8).

### Stage 8 — GameGold integration
**Goal:** the experimental crypto layer (blackcoin-more fork, 1:1 with in-game gold, temple-faucet cold-start, manual exchange initially).
**Why last:** explicitly post-MVP, and its whole point — emergent market value — **needs market participants**, i.e. a living player economy first. Also the highest external/infra risk (node on Orange Pi, upstream Bitcoin Core rebase maintenance).
**Dependencies:** **Stage 4 (in-game currency) — hard prerequisite**, since GameGold bridges 1:1 to in-game gold; plus a functioning player economy (barter ✅) and enough players for exchange to be meaningful.
**Pillars:** experimental economy.
**Design docs already exist:** `GameGold_Design.md`, `GameGold_Blockchain_Platform.md`.
**Framing guardrail:** hobby/experiment, speculation discouraged, value set by free market.

## Parallel / opportunistic backlog (off the critical path)

- **Epic A — timed actions** ✅ *(merged to `main`, `34b2ee4`, 2026-10-17)* — the shared home for "this takes time and can be interrupted". - **Recipe mastery & craft disciplines** — a per-recipe completed-craft counter (`RecipeMasteryHandler` as a `@lazy_property` on Character, mirroring `world/skill_xp.py`; no backfill), written from exactly one production call-site: `MongooseCraftRecipe.do_craft`, inside the `_should_produce` branch, so successes and criticals count and fumbles do not. It closes two measured gaps: `_can_transmit` reads *global* Craft today, so a smith who has never made a waterskin may teach it; and every recipe rolls the same `craft` trait, so specialisation has no mechanical existence at all. A new one-per-recipe `discipline` class attribute groups the catalogue for display (`recipes` gains discipline-summary and discipline-list modes) and carries cosmetic titles ("Journeyman Weaver"); mechanical cross-recipe aggregation is **rejected** — it would reintroduce the generic-competence gap the counter exists to fix. `discipline` is deliberately *not* `PROFESSIONS`. Grinding is self-limiting: common recipes cannot be taught, so mastering twine is worth nothing. *(Consider elevating to a numbered stage once the recipe catalogue exists.)*

- **Legend character creation** — replace the current hardcoded static traits/skills (every player identical) with proper Legend Adventurer Creation: roll *or allocate* the seven Characteristics (STR/CON/SIZ/DEX/INT/POW/CHA) → derive attributes (HP, damage modifier, Magic Points, Strike Rank, Improvement Roll modifier) → layer **Cultural Background** (Barbarian/Civilised/Nomad/Primitive: Common-skill bonuses, Combat Styles, Advanced Skills, starting money) + **Profession** + **free skill points** + community/family + starting gear. - **Food professions — farming · fishing · livestock** — the non-combat player roles the economy is missing, and the only food sources that *scale* (hunting and foraging are bounded by respawn by construction). Legend already carries the skills: `Craft (Farming)` with `Lore (Regional)` secondary, `Craft (Fishing)`, `Lore (Animal)`. **One item, not three, because they share a failure mode:** five food sources with overlapping outputs collapse each other's prices, so each source needs *exclusive* products decided once across the whole set — grain and linen to farming, wool and bulk leather to livestock, fish oil and preservable salt fish to fishing, and so on. Split into three entries, that decision gets made three times by accident. **Architecture, settled ahead of code:** never tick crops or animals — store `planted_at` / `last_fed` and derive state on read, the same lazy no-backfill shape `world/currency.py` already uses; 20 players × 12 plots is 240 pointless subscriptions otherwise, and offline time falls out for free. One `FarmPlot` object owning a slot dict rather than an object per seed (one attribute to version, one lock, one `at_object_delete`); one `Pen` holding the herd as an aggregate (adults, juveniles, condition, fodder) rather than per-animal AI. **Every one of them needs a real sink or it is a faucet:** field fertility and depletion plus a seed-vs-food tradeoff for farming, fodder consumption and density-scaled disease for livestock. Skill check at *harvest*, not at planting — critical yields superior quality, fumble halves the crop, which is Legend's craft resolution unchanged. **Ordering:** after Epic A, because plant and harvest are timed actions and building the wait mechanic a third time by hand is precisely what Epic A exists to stop; after the crafting tool slice, because farming without tools is a command with no economy under it. Fishing is the cheapest of the three (it reuses the existing harvest-node pattern, plus passive traps on the same lazy-eval trick) and can run in parallel; livestock is the most expensive and should go last.

- **Temperature & exposure** — a cold/heat survival gauge alongside hunger, thirst and fatigue, coupled to season, time of day, weather and worn clothing. This is the change that converts the environment systems from scenery into mechanics: today seasons gate weather, weather gates descriptions, and nothing reads either. It is **stage-sized and belongs here rather than in `BACKLOG.md`** — the cheap weather work (inertia, season-dependent day length, regional weather) is filed there, and the line between them is exactly the scope rule: those are function bodies, this is a new trait, a new coupling to `ContribClothing`, a new death mode, and a migration surface on every existing character. - **Godot 2.5D client** — an optional graphical front end, to reach players who will not start a text-only game. Technically clear: `evennia.contrib.base_systems.godotwebsocket` (verified at source 2026-09-05) is a Portal plugin on its own websocket port (default 4008) that translates Evennia's `|`-colour codes to BBCode and forwards **all OOB data** — anything passed as `msg(text, **kwargs)` — as structured data to the client. - **Disease system** — seasonal, ties to gametime + survival. Pairs naturally with herbalism.

- **[RESOLVED] Death-consequence policy — respawn with consequence, everything drops to the body, currency is never deleted, owner-first retrieval, wounds survive death (2026-10-17).** *(Filed at Rev 1 as **[OPEN] Death-consequence policy**; the original text stands as history, the decision follows it.)* Stage 0 built the *mechanic* (HP 0 → death → player-corpse), but the *consequence* is undecided: permadeath or not, item loss on death, corpse-retrieval runs? It's simultaneously a stake (combat) and a sink (economy), and it interacts with coin-as-objects (Stage 4) — decide it deliberately, not implicitly in a commit. **Timing:** it is a player contract, not only a mechanic — settle it *before* the first real test cohort, not during Stage 5. Retro-fitting item loss onto players who started without it is a different act from shipping with it. **Epic A note (2026-10-17):** the line an interrupted craft prints, *"your materials are untouched"* (Timed Actions decomp T17), is true of the craft but not of a death, because `at_character_death()` moves the inventory to the corpse straight after the interrupt (decomp §10) — re-read it when item loss is decided. **Sequencing (2026-10-17, alpha decision C):** this is the next decision session, taken before code on either the alpha track or Stage 5 — it is the only alpha prerequisite that is both dependency-ordered (after character creation) and time-bound (before the first cohort), and Stage 5's hit-location sub-question (a) cannot be answered without it. **Decision (2026-10-17, death-policy session) — five points, each answering a question this entry or one of its neighbours asked.** **(i) Not permadeath.** `PolishedWorld_Hunting_Decomposition.md` §12 settled this at feature altitude on 2026-07-05 (*"respawn with consequence, not deletion"*) and `at_character_death()`'s docstring has said *NOT permadeath* since; it is ratified here at roadmap altitude so that it is a decision rather than a description of current behaviour. The reason is pillar 1: a skill percentage is the one thing Stage 4.5 made expensive, and a dead crafter is a supplier removed from a market with no NPC vendor to fill the gap. A per-character hardcore flag was rejected as a second ruleset to balance against one cohort. A *dying state* (H7.4 — bleed-out, First-Aid rescue) is **not** this question: it changes the trigger of death, not its consequence, and lives with Stage 5 and *Two-stage healing* in `docs/BACKLOG.md`. **(ii) Item loss — everything drops to the body.** All non-`soulbound` inventory moves into the `PlayerCorpse`, worn items stripped first, as shipped (H7.1). `soulbound` is an **admin tool for quest and admin objects, never equipment**: the door Hunting decomp §13 left open — *tag basic clothing/tools later if loss-frustration becomes a problem* — is closed, because every soulbound garment is a garment nobody sells, and the respawned, naked player is the one recurring demand for clothing that the alpha catalogue (11 of its 44 `[DATA]` items) can count on. **Coin goes with the body, not to the room:** `CoinPile` (Stage 5, Currency decomposition S4-3) materialises *inside* the corpse like every other belonging — one container, one lock, one retrieval run. `PolishedWorld_Economic_Philosophy.md` §5 said *"drops to the room"* while the code and `commands/currency_commands.py` said *"with the corpse"*; §5 is corrected (Rev 4) to match. **Currency is never deleted by any cleanup path.** This answers `docs/BACKLOG.md`'s *`CoinPile` vs the corpse's atomic content delete*, which was scheduled for Stage 5 and is decided here instead, because it is a policy and not a `CoinPile` detail: when a cleanup must remove a `CoinPile` — the corpse crumbling at expiry, or a wilderness room being recycled in Stage 6 — the pile is **transferred to the Treasury with a ledger entry**, never deleted. That keeps §5's single-exit rule and the `@economy audit` invariant intact, means no pile can ever lie in a room that may be reset, and is thematically right: ownerless gold returns to the temple that funds the faucet. The cost is treasure-hunting *after* the window, not during it. Gear is different — it has a source, crafting — so the atomic delete of everything else at expiry **stands** as the loot sink. **(iii) Corpse retrieval — a run, counted in days, owner-first.** The corpse lies where the character fell and holds the loot for a recovery window (today `_PLAYER_EXPIRY_HOURS = 288` game hours ≈ 72 real hours at `TIME_FACTOR = 4`, environment-scaled); the player contract promises *days*, not a number, so the constant stays a calibration knob. **Looting is owner-only for a grace window, then open to anyone** — H7.3b's `get_from:true()` becomes a time-conditioned lock. Open looting from the first second is the honest reading of §5 (*"whoever finds your remains"*), but in an alpha without combat the only player-on-player loss is looting a starved body, and it would fall on the players still learning to eat; the grace turns a first-cohort death into *run back*, not *robbed*. The grace length is a calibration constant, not part of the contract. **(iv) Wounds coexist with `DeathWeakness`, and survive death** — the sub-question the hit-location entry below left for this one. `DeathWeakness` stays what it is: a timed, self-expiring respawn debuff (its `fatigue_rate` retarget remains a separate BACKLOG item). Wound tiers are character state (Rev 20), and **the *maimed* and *severed* tiers persist through respawn**; death restores Hit Points and therefore clears *disabled*, which RAW already makes temporary. The rule exists because of a hazard the shipped code has: `at_character_death()` sets `health.current = health.max`, and if respawn also cleared maiming, starving to death would be the cheapest surgeon in the world — the one NPC healer pillar 1 forbids. Filed in `docs/BACKLOG.md` as a Stage 5-blocked change to the restore block. **(v) The death message carries the contract.** An interrupted craft prints *"your materials are untouched"* (T17) and a death prints *"You have died."* one line later, with the materials already in the corpse. T17 stays true of the craft and unchanged; the death line gains the contract instead — where the body is, that it keeps the belongings for days, that the weakness passes — plus a `help death` topic. The prose sites this decision falsifies were counted before writing (Testing Reference §11c): **seven sites in four files** — the death line and the `at_character_death()` docstring (`typeclasses/characters.py`), the crumble line and the `PlayerCorpse` docstrings (`typeclasses/corpse.py`), T17's `interrupt_msg` (`commands/crafting_commands.py`, stays), and the module docstring of `commands/currency_commands.py`. They are listed in the BACKLOG entry that implements the contract; nothing in code changes in this Rev. **Consequences elsewhere:** Economic Philosophy §5 reworded (Rev 4); Currency decomposition S4-3 gains a pointer (Rev 8); BACKLOG Rev 39 rewrites the `CoinPile` entry, aligns *Bank / post office*, and adds two entries (the contract surfaces; the restore block). The alpha's fifth prerequisite is settled.
- **[RESOLVED] Hit locations & wound permanence (Stage 5) — asymmetric locational HP, three wound tiers, permanence feature-gated (2026-09-09).** Legend divides every body into Hit Locations and this roadmap has cited them as Stage 5 alignment since Rev 1, but the *data model* was never chosen. It is not a combat detail: it is read by the shipped `apply_health_damage()` chokepoint, by per-location armour AP (seven locations = seven craft orders per suit, which is pillar 1 with depth), and by the `ContribClothing` slot taxonomy — three systems, so retrofitting it after the epic starts is the expensive path. **Decided:** (1) **Asymmetric HP** — players get Legend's seven locational Hit Points; creatures and NPCs get **General Hit Points + Critical Wound rating**. That is not a house rule — it is Legend's own *Optional Combat Rules For Non-Player Characters*, written for exactly this bookkeeping problem, so full fidelity lands where the player feels it and a single integer lands where only the server cares. (2) **Three wound tiers** — *disabled* (RAW Serious Wound: location unusable until back to positive HP; temporary), *maimed* (long-lived; cleared by another player's Healing/surgery), *severed* (Major Wound to a limb; ritual/high magic or a prosthesis). (3) **Permanence feature-gated at ship** — the severed tier is implemented but switched off until a cure exists in the world. A launch with no healers and no magic would permanently maim the first cohort with no route out, and in a game whose core loop is crafting a one-armed character is retired, not challenged; without levels there is no growing out of it. Gate rather than defer, so no migration debt accrues. **Why permanence is worth having at all:** the drama is the bleed-out window, not the stump — Legend gives an untreated severed location `CON+POW` minutes before death from blood loss and shock, which is a downed player, a running clock and a rescue, and it turns Healing/First Aid into a *service another player sells* (pillar 1) plus a prosthesis market for crafters. **Constraints that fall out, recorded because they are easy to violate silently:** locational HP must permit **negative** values — Major Wound is defined as negative HP equal to the location's starting HP, and `CounterTrait` clamps at `min` without complaining, so a default `min=0` makes the tier unreachable rather than rare; lasting wound state is **not** a buff (`buffs.add()` increments stacks rather than setting them, and a buff reads as temporary) — it is character state written through the existing single-writer chokepoint, whose signature gains a location; and the `health` gauge becomes **derived on read** from the locations rather than a second writer (P-2). **Open sub-questions, for the Stage 5 design session:** (a) whether wound state *replaces* or coexists with the `DeathWeakness` debuff — this one is **not** answered here: it is part of **[OPEN] death-consequence policy**, which Rev 19 records as due *before* Stage 5 planning, and half a player contract settled inside a combat entry is how contracts get made implicitly; **answered 2026-10-17 in that entry, (iv): they coexist — wound state persists through respawn (*maimed*, *severed*), `DeathWeakness` stays a timed respawn debuff;** (b) the non-combat damage location policy (see the Stage 5 entry); (c) armour-layering AP stacking and the `ContribClothing`-slot ↔ Hit-Location mapping, both needing *Arms of Legend* source verification before decomposition; (d) **severed stays PvE-only even after the gate opens** — permanent maiming is a harassment vector the moment PvP exists, which feeds the entry below.
- **[RESOLVED] Organ-level damage model rejected — Legend's wound tiers already carry it (2026-09-09).** Proposed (via another Evennia developer's implementation): a per-organ layer beneath the Hit Locations — roughly 28 organs and bones, each with its own HP and status, each mapped to a hit/clothing location, with named organs wired to functions (brain → consciousness, lungs → breathing), producing a triage layer and a surgery layer, plus transplants. **Declined**, for four reasons worth keeping. (1) **It is redundant against the ruleset we already chose.** Legend's Serious Wound already renders a limb useless, drops what the arm holds, puts a leg-hit prone and knocks an Abdomen/Chest/Head hit unconscious; Major Wound already maims or severs, already starts a `CON+POW`-minute bleed-out clock, and the rulebook already says most Major Wounds need surgery or major magic. The organ layer restates that in higher resolution — it adds *nouns*, not player decisions. (2) **It multiplies an unsolved mapping.** Rev 20's open sub-question (c) — armour AP stacking and the `ContribClothing`-slot ↔ Hit-Location mapping — is unsolved at **seven** locations; a sub-taxonomy of twenty-eight would be solved *under* an unsolved one. Same for the shipped `apply_health_damage()` chokepoint, which Rev 20 already gives a location argument: giving it an organ argument as well makes every hit two resolutions and two writes per combatant per swing (P-2, and a real cost at ten simultaneous players). (3) **The failure mode is the one Rev 20 already gated for.** Permanence was feature-gated because a world with no healers has no route out of a maiming; an organ model *deepens* that hole — a player with a destroyed liver and nobody online to operate is a retired character, not a challenged one, and pillar 1 forbids the NPC surgeon that makes RimWorld's version work. (4) **Transplants would make player organs tradeable items**, which is a harassment vector before it is an economy, and it collides with Rev 20 sub-question (d) (severed stays PvE-only). **Kept from the proposal:** the triage/surgery split, which is the genuinely new idea and is filed as a BACKLOG entry (*Health & Injury*), and it lands exactly where Rev 20 already pointed — Healing/First Aid as a service another player sells. **Also noted as free:** per-location flavour variation in hit messages ("the blade grates along your ribs") buys the felt detail of an organ model at zero persistent state, because it is presentation, not damage.
- **[RESOLVED] A graphical client is additive, never authoritative (2026-09-09).** Settled now, years before any client exists, because it constrains what commands may do *today*. **The text stream stays canonical:** anything visible in a Godot client must be doable in telnet — no mechanic may exist that only the graphical player can reach, or the "optional" client becomes mandatory and every telnet player is playing a worse game. **What follows for current work:** when a command has spatial or environmental state to report, send it as OOB kwargs *alongside* the text, not instead of it. That costs almost nothing today and is the identical preparation a web-client map would need, so it is not speculative work for a client that may never be built. **Two things are locked so the throwaway PoC leaves something behind:** the client lives in a **separate repository** (`PolishedWorld-client`) — it is a different application with a different release cadence, and vendoring it here would make every tileset commit noise in the MUD's history; and the OOB payload **field names `survival` and `map_update`** are fixed now, so server-side emission written before the client exists does not have to be rewritten when it does. Everything else about the payload schema is explicitly provisional. **What stays open:** whether rooms get coordinates at all is Stage 6's question, not this entry's — recorded here as an input to that decision rather than a presumption of its outcome.
- **[OPEN] Combat: PvE-only first vs PvP** — affects fairness/design scope. **Note (2026-10-17, death-consequence policy):** two PvP-facing protections already stand before this is decided — the severed tier stays PvE-only (hit-location entry, (d)) and a player corpse is owner-only for a grace window before open looting (death policy, (iii)). Whichever way this goes, those two are inputs to it, not consequences of it.
- **[OPEN] Combat timing model** — real-time-with-cooldowns is the stance; needs explicit multiplayer-fairness design before decomposition.
- **[OPEN] GameGold trigger** — tie launch to a player-count milestone, not a calendar date. **Anchored (2026-10-17, alpha decision A):** not before the alpha closes; the player count is set at alpha close-out, when there is a cohort to count.
- **[OPEN] Character creation: rolled vs point-buy** — Legend offers both random (3D6 / 2D6+6) and a non-random points method. Pure random rolls invite re-roll abuse in a MUD (recreate until good stats); point-buy or a constrained standard array is the multiplayer-fair choice and stays Legend-faithful. Also: map cultural/professional skill grants onto the *locked skill taxonomy* (hunting/craft/…), not Legend's full skill list.
- **[DEFERRED] Procedural world generation** — hand-built frontier + scaling hooks first.
