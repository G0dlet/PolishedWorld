## 1. Why this feature

Pillar 1 measures emergent prices, and prices need geography: a thing must cost more where it is used than where it is gathered, or carrying is free and no one is paid to carry. Today one character carries any quantity anywhere — `enc` has no reader (grep of `commands/`, `typeclasses/`, `world/`: zero hits outside `world/prototypes.py`). This feature gives `enc` a reader, gives STR and SIZ their first reader in the alpha, and gives the three craftable containers (sack, backpack, saddlebag — AC4.2) a use. It must be on `main` before *Persistent alpha instance* opens: objects spawned there before a reader exists would otherwise be carried without limit until a migration (roadmap (E), BACKLOG *Weight and encumbrance* trigger).

## 2. Decisions locked 2026-10-26 (WD1–WD8)

**WD1 — The limit is STR+SIZ, as written (Legend p.86).** No scaling, no flat cap. With Points Build (CC1) the limit spans 12–36; a character made through chargen has a limit set by its Points Build; development characters (not migrated, CC7) and the test suite's characters keep STR 10, SIZ 10 (`static`, `typeclasses/characters.py:197, 218`) and a limit of 20. The limit reads the stats live, so chargen changes it without a migration. Measured against the catalogue: the heaviest item weighs 2 (`saddlebag`, `bone_armour`, `quarterstaff`, `spear`, `milling_stones`, `tent`), eleven weigh 1, thirty weigh 0 (`world/prototypes.py`, 47 fields), and `wood` and `stone` are one object per unit at `enc` 1 — so the limit bites on bulk, which is exactly pillar 1's case: a weak character is overloaded by the thirteenth log, a strong one carries 36. *Rejected:* **A**, a scaled limit (a second knob, and it makes the Legend and *Arms of Legend* ENC columns wrong in both directions); **B**, a flat limit (removes STR and SIZ's only reader in the alpha).

**WD2 — Two bands and a ceiling: free to the limit, *Overloaded* to twice it, refused above (an adaptation, not RAW).** Legend's rule is a soft limit: *Overloaded* is a penalty, never a refusal. The project adds a ceiling at **2 × (STR+SIZ)**, Legend's own figure for beasts of burden (p.86: *never more than double*; core text line ~6292) borrowed for people. *Why the ceiling:* the band's costs are a skill penalty and a fatigue cost (WD3), and fatigue at zero has no consequence today (*Fatigue-exhaustion consequence* is OPEN), so without a ceiling the alpha's hauler pays nothing until that entry ships. The ceiling prices distance **in trips** from the first day — the OSRS and Tibia model the chargen decomposition already adopts (CC1's framing) — while the band stays Legend-faithful and gains teeth later. The refusal is on *receiving* only: `Character.at_pre_object_receive` returns False above the ceiling, for `get`, `give`, `get … from` and `put`; `drop` and movement are never refused (Epic A D1: a move is always allowed). That is the criterion the BACKLOG entry set — the cost makes haulage a service, it never locks the player. *Rejected:* **A**, Legend's soft limit alone (prices nothing before *Fatigue-exhaustion consequence*, which would make that entry an eighth prerequisite in practice); **B**, a hard limit at STR+SIZ (deletes the *Overloaded* state, and with it Stage 5's −20 % on weapon tests). **Consequence:** *Fatigue-exhaustion consequence* is neither inside this feature nor a dependency of it (the question its BACKLOG entry and this one asked). It stays OPEN; W3.2's per-room cost is wired now and becomes a reason to rest when that entry ships.

**WD3 — The band's cost: −20 % on physical tests, and a fatigue cost per room moved. No buff, no rate multiplier.** *This reverses the direction recorded 2026-10-18 (BACKLOG Rev 41: an `Overloaded` buff with a `fatigue_rate` mult, recomputed each tick).* Two reasons. **(a)** A rate multiplier prices *time*; a cost per room prices *distance*, and distance is pillar 1's quantity — a loaded character standing in a workshop is not working harder than an unloaded one, and Legend's *Movement halved* translates to a room-based world as *every room costs*. The −20 % on Fatigue tests has nothing to attach to: the project has no activity tests (Legend p.89), only a gauge. **(b)** A buff is a cache: dropping an item leaves it stale until the next tick, and a 30-minute tick (`settings.py:91`) is a long time to be wrongly overloaded. Summing `contents` on demand is the lazy, single-writer pattern (P-2): Evennia keeps `contents` in memory (the contents cache), the sum is O(n) over tens of objects, and nothing is stored. The −20 % is Legend's own (p.86, *all tests that require physical actions … most tests that have DEX or STR as a Characteristic*). It needs a seam that does not exist: every caller passes its own `modifier` to `skill_check()` (`world/skillcheck.py:46`; six call sites, no character-level modifier). W3.1 adds `Character.physical_modifier()` → −20 or 0, and the physical callers add it: every Craft test (DEX+INT — `characters.py:390`: craft, disassemble, repair, harvest) and hunting's opposed check. Forage and scribing are excluded: forage is Survival, CON+POW in Legend, scribing is Lore, INT × 2 — the *exceptions* the rule allows. *(Rev 5: both premises are false against live source. Scribing rolls Craft (`crafting_commands.py:777, 823`) and is in; forage makes no skill test, so there is nothing to exclude. See W3.1 Q1–Q2 and OQ2.)* The same seam carries the Fatigue-level penalties when that entry ships. *Rejected:* **A**, the rate-multiplier buff (reasons above); **B**, a movement delay (a timed move is a new mechanism, and Epic A's slot interrupts on move — a delayed move would have to refuse, which D1 forbids); **C**, a forbidding band (locks the player — the criterion).

**WD4 — Twenty items at `enc` 0 count as 1 ENC, wherever they are carried.** `enc` 0 means *too light to score*, never weightless (BACKLOG annotation 2026-10-26): `zero_enc_count // 20` is added to the sum, counted over the whole carried tree, with or without a container. Legend's *so long as the Adventurer has a suitable means of carrying them* is plausibility, not a mechanic; making it one is a second knob and a special case for the character holding nineteen stones in two hands. The test the BACKLOG entry demands — twenty items at `enc` 0 count as 1 ENC — is W1.1's, with nineteen counting as 0.

**WD5 — Containers hold ENC, not items; contents count fully for the carrier; no nesting.** `typeclasses/containers.py::Container(ContribContainer)` overrides `at_pre_put_in` (the documented point, Evennia `contrib/game_systems/containers/containers.py:83`; the shipped check is `len(self.contents) >= self.capacity`, line 99) to compare the container's carried ENC plus the item's against `capacity` in ENC. Capacities from *Arms of Legend*: backpack 20 (p.28), sack 10 (p.29, *Sack, Large*), saddlebag 15 (p.30, *each saddlebag*). A container does not lighten its contents (Legend lists the container's own ENC; nothing says the contents weigh less), so the carrier's sum is recursive. A container refuses another container (one check in `at_pre_put_in`), so the recursion is one level deep in practice — the reader is still written recursively, because a corpse is a container-shaped object and a future one might nest. `CmdPut` (`containers.py:227`) is added alone to the character cmdset, as `CmdContainerGet` already is (`commands/default_cmdsets.py:180`; *Rev 5:* since W2.2 that slot holds `CmdGet` from `commands/inventory_commands.py`, at line 181); `ContainerCmdSet` as a whole stays out because `CmdContainerLook` collides with the seasonal `look` (BACKLOG *Full container support*, folded into this feature). *What a container is for, with WD4 counting without one:* it is the haulage unit — `give sack to <hauler>` moves ten ENC in one command and one hook, and a dropped sack is one object on the floor, not twenty. *Rejected:* nesting (recursive capacity checks for no alpha use); `capacity` as items (the contrib's default — a sack of twenty logs would weigh the same as a sack of twenty bullets).

**WD6 — Crafting and foraging honour the ceiling by setting the surplus down.** Forage already moves its yield with `item.move_to(caller, quiet=True)` (`commands/foraging_commands.py:138`), so the W2.1 hook applies; when it refuses, the item is moved to the room with *"… is too much to carry; you set it down."* — no refund, because the node is already taken (lesson 93). Crafting assigns `obj.location = self.crafter` directly (`world/crafting_base.py:324`), **bypassing every hook**, and is changed to the same `move_to` with the same fallback. *(Rev 3: harvest, inscribe and scribe spawn their product unplaced and then `move_to(caller)`, so they take the same fallback; W5.1 lists all five.)* The corpse case needs no decision: `Corpse` carries `get:false()` (`typeclasses/corpse.py:80`), so a body is never carried — death is a retrieval run, as the death-consequence policy (ii) intends, and looting it item by item goes through the same receive hook.

**WD7 — The wallet weighs nothing; `CoinPile`'s ENC is Stage 5's.** Coin is an integer in copper on the character (S4-2, `world/currency.py`), not objects, and this feature does not change that (no-backfill, Economic Philosophy §4). `CoinPile` does not exist in the code today (grep: no class); when Stage 5 materialises it, it is an item prototype and AD8's rule gives it an `enc` like any other — recorded as an annotation on BACKLOG *`CoinPile` vs the corpse's atomic content delete*.

**WD8 — No Source/Sink Ledger row.** Weight creates nothing and destroys nothing; it is a price on distance. The ledger renders sources and sinks (lesson 103: a rendered field is a claim), and a row for a mechanic that is neither would be a false one. The economic claim lives in this document's §1 and the roadmap's (E).

## 5. Component W1 — The reader (Claude)

### Task W1.1 — `world/encumbrance.py`: pure functions over objects
- **Goal:** One module that answers *how much does this carry* and *what state is this character in*, with no state of its own.
- **Dependencies:** None.
- **Approach:** `item_enc(obj) -> int` reads `obj.attributes.get("enc", default=0)` and coerces to `int` (a missing field is 0 — the 20 `create_object` test objects and any builder-made object weigh nothing rather than raising); a value `int()` refuses (`None`, `"heavy"`) or a negative one reads 0 and logs a warning naming the object and the value, because a negative `enc` would make its carrier lighter (locked 2026-10-29). `carried_enc(holder) -> int` walks `holder.contents` recursively, summing `item_enc` and counting objects at 0, then adds `zero_count // 20` (WD4); the walk ignores characters (`inherits_from(obj, DefaultCharacter)`; `has_account` would miss an unpuppeted one) and exits (`obj.destination`), and stops at a depth of 4 as a guard — depth is 2 in practice (WD5): an object at depth 4 (the holder's own contents are depth 1) counts its own `enc` but is not descended into, and the stop logs a warning, because reaching it means WD5's no-nesting check failed (locked 2026-10-29). `enc_limit(char) -> int` is `stats.str.value + stats.siz.value`. `encumbrance_state(char) -> str` returns the module constant `FREE` at or below the limit, `OVERLOADED` above it up to `2 * limit`, and `CEILING` above that (the strings `"free"`, `"overloaded"`, `"ceiling"`, so W2 and W3 never spell a state; locked 2026-10-29). ## 13. Consequences worth reading twice

- **The direction of 2026-10-18 is reversed (WD3).** A reader of BACKLOG Rev 41's annotation expects a buff and a rate multiplier; neither exists. The annotation is corrected in BACKLOG Rev 50 rather than left as history, because it reads as an instruction.
- **Characters carry different limits now.** Development characters (not migrated, CC7) and the test suite's characters carry 20; a character made through chargen carries 12–36, so the same protocol on a 12 or a 36 reads differently. Protocols state the limit they were run at.
- **The weight code does not need character creation.** Order (F) was about the alpha, not the graph (§4): the limit reads whatever STR and SIZ are, so neither chargen nor any later change to the characteristics needs a migration here.
- **A missing `enc` is silent at runtime.** The reader's *missing = 0* (W1.1) keeps eight test files untouched and makes a forgotten field a weightless item in play. Test (g) is the guard, and it covers prototypes only; an object created by hand in the persistent instance is weightless until a builder sets `enc`. Named in the Testing Reference as a builder rule.
- **The ceiling is the first haulage.** With `wood` at `enc` 1, a 20-limit character hauls at most 40 logs in one trip and is overloaded for the last twenty rooms of it. A second character, or a second trip, is the price of distance from the first day — and the sack makes the hand-off one command.
- **Containers do not lighten.** A player who expects a backpack to *add* capacity (as in most MMOs) will find it adds organisation and a hand-off unit, and costs 1 ENC. `help put` says so in one sentence.
- **Prose sites.** Three named in W6.1 before code; expect three-to-four more (the epic pattern in the gotcha file).
